## 1. Objective

Build a timetable scheduler that ingests the department's subject, teacher, room and
time data, and produces a conflict-free weekly timetable that satisfies every rule in
`guildlines.pdf`, in three delivery forms: **local**, **mock-backend**, **website**.

Success = a schedule where all hard constraints hold, all required periods are
placed, and the result can be exported/inspected per teacher, room, group and
subject.

1. **Printed totals do not reconcile.** `ปวช.1` shows `50` and `ปวช.3` shows `48` in the

2. **No teacher↔subject assignment exists.** The `ครู` sheet lists only name, position and

5. **Inconsistent code formatting** (`21909 - 1001` vs `21909-1001`, `20000 -1603`) — normalize on ingest.
6. **General-room labels are inconsistent with subjects** (e.g. M.2 `ภาษาอังกฤษเพื่องานช่างอุตสาหกรรม`

## 3. Normalized data model

```
Level(id, name, max_period)                 # ปวช.1 → 9, ปวช.2/3 → 11
Group(id, level_id, name)                   # ทค.1/1, ทค.1/2, ...
Subject(id, code, name, periods, kind, level_id, room_class, pin_day, pin_start)
   kind       ∈ {THEORY, PRACTICAL, ACTIVITY}
   room_class ∈ {GENERAL:<name>, COM, LAB, COM_OR_LAB, DEPARTMENT}
Teacher(id, name, role, min_load, max_load, unavailability[])
Room(id, name, type ∈ {GENERAL, COM, LAB})
Timeslot(day ∈ 1..5, period ∈ 1..11)
Session(subject_id, group_id, teacher_id, room_id, day, start_period, length)
```

Sessions are **blocks**, not single periods: `length` = subject periods, contiguous
(rule 9 forbids splitting). Theory/activity = one block shared by both groups of a
level. Practical = one block **per group** (rule 4).

### 3.1 Attendance model (rule 4)

- **Theory (`ทฤษฎี`)** — both groups of the level attend together → 1 session per subject.
- **Practical (`ปฏิบัติ`)** — groups split → 2 sessions per subject (group 1 and group 2),
  doubling room and teacher demand.
- **Activity (`กิจกรรม…`, `ลูก scout`)** — theory-type, both groups together.

## 4. Constraint catalogue (from `guildlines.pdf`)

### Hard constraints (violation = invalid schedule)

| ID | Rule | Guideline # |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | ปวช.1 sessions end by period 9; ปวช.2/3 by period 11 | 1 |
| H2 | Teachers have no break period (may teach through lunch) | 1 |
| H3 | Exactly 1 lunch hour per level per day, at period 4, 5 or 6 | 2 |
| H4 | Activity/scout courses at Thu 6–7 | 3 |
| H5 | Theory merged for both groups; practical split per group | 4 |
| H6 | Head of dept & head of work: 24–28 h/week | 5 |
| H7 | Regular teachers: 30–38 h/week | 5 |
| H8 | Computer rooms limited to COM1–COM4; labs to LAB1–LAB2 | 6, 7 |
| H9 | General subjects use external classrooms, reusable | 8 |
| H10 | No split periods — each subject is one contiguous block | 9 |
| H11 | General subjects excluded from dept teacher hour totals | 10 |
| H12 | No teacher double-booking | 11 |
| H13 | No room double-booking | 12 |
| H14 | No group double-booking | 13 |
| H15 | Period counts must match the data exactly | 14 |
| H16 | No scheduling in teacher/room unavailability (e.g. ครู A free Wed 1–4) | 15 |
| H17 | Never exceed max teaching load | 16 |
| H18 | Every level starts at period 1 | 17 |
| H19 | Mon–Fri only | 18 |
| H20 | Every level has class every day | 19 |

Implied (needed to make H18/H20/H3 operable, and echoed by the source data):
no empty period between classes except the lunch hour; practical blocks must not
straddle lunch; lunch position is a per-level per-day decision variable.

### Objective (soft)

1. Zero hard violations (dominant term).
2. Compact group timetables: minimise idle gaps, keep days balanced.
3. Fair teacher load inside the allowed band, preferring mid-band.
4. Room utilisation preference COM before LAB for `COM_OR_LAB` subjects.
5. Deterministic, reproducible output for demo purposes.

## 5. Capacity and feasibility pre-check

Derived from the data rows (not the printed totals).

| Level | Rows | General h (incl. activity) | Dept practical h | Per-group periods | Max period | Capacity (5 days − 5 lunches) | Slack | Theory sessions | Practical sessions (2 groups) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ปวช.1 | 12 | 12 | 24 | 36 | 9 | 40 | 4 | 6 | 12 |
| ปวช.2 | 11 | 6 | 28 (+3 dept theory) | 37 | 11 | 50 | 13 | 4 | 14 |
| ปวช.3 | 10 | 9 | 21 (+4 dept theory) | 34 | 11 | 50 | 16 | 5 | 10 |

**Teacher load.** Department teaching hours (practical × 2 groups + dept theory):
`ปวช.1 48 + ปวช.2 59 + ปวช.3 46 = 153 h/week`.
Available band: 24+24+30+30+30 = **138 h min**, 28+28+38+38+38 = **170 h max**.
Feasible with **17 h headroom**, but only in the upper half of the range: A 28 + B 28
leaves 97 h for C/D/E ≈ 32.3 h each (allowed 30–38). Activity courses (3 × 2 h) add
6 h if they count as dept load → 159 h, still ≤ 170. **Open question:** do activity
courses count toward teacher hours (rule 10 excludes only `รายวิชาสามัญ`)?

**Room demand.** Practical room-hours: COM **122 h**, LAB **16 h**, flexible COM/LAB
**8 h** (+3 h theory `คณิตศาสตร์คอมพิวเตอร์`). Capacity: COM 4 rooms × 5 days × 11
periods = 220 slots; LAB 110 slots. Demand ≈ 146 h against 330 slots — comfortable in
aggregate; the binding constraint is the concentration in Wednesday/Friday windows, not
total capacity.

**Practical block arithmetic (the tight constraint).** `ปวช.1` needs six 4-hour blocks
per group and ends at period 9. With pins Mon 1–4, Tue 1–4 + 6–7, Thu 6–7, and lunch at
P4/5/6, the usable 4-hour windows are: Mon 1 block, Wed 2, Thu 1, Fri 2 — Tuesday yields
**none**. 36 periods against 40 slots with only 6 practical windows means the ปวช.1 solver
has near-zero slack; expect this level to drive backtracking. Mitigation: treat ปวช.1 as
the first-solved level and allow lunch placement to move between P4/P5/P6.

**Known risk:** 5 teachers covering 153 h at an average of 30.6 h each, with two of them
capped at 28 h, means the search space for *who teaches what* is thin. If the department
adds a 6th teacher or lowers C/D/E's minimum, difficulty drops sharply.

## 6. Solution approach

Two-stage, plan-then-act (auditable, and the reduction in conflicts can be shown):

**Stage A — assign.** Decide teacher-per-session and room-per-session skeleton:
- 153 h over 5 teachers inside role bands (H6/H7/H17), respecting ครู A's Wed 1–4 block.
- Practical pairs to COM/LAB by `room_class`; `COM_OR_LAB` prefers COM (H8).

**Stage B — place.** Place blocks on the Mon–Fri × P1–P11 grid:
- Model as CSP: variables = block start slot + room; domains pre-filtered by pins,
  level max period, lunch options, and unavailability.
- Prune with forward-checking + MRV (most-constrained block first: 5-hour game course,
  then 4-hour practicals, then pinned theory).
- Optimise with local search / simulated annealing over (day, start period) moves,
  cost = weighted hard violations × 1000 + gap penalty + balance penalty.
- Optional exact fallback: exhaustive depth-first search with conflict-directed
  backjumping over the same constraint model, in plain JavaScript — no native solver
  or second runtime. At this size (≈36 practical sessions + 12 theory sessions per
  level) it is used to prove that a level is infeasible, not to produce the schedule.
- Never emit a schedule with hard violations: if unsolved, report the minimal
  conflicting set and the blocking rule (required by the evaluation, and by the
  department's own note that genuinely contradictory rules should be judged on
  analysis rather than silently dropped).

**Determinism:** fixed RNG seed per run; the seed and the cost trace are logged so a demo
can be replayed.

## 7. System architecture (three run modes)

```
src/ingest/     PDF text + XLSX readers → canonical JSON package (implemented)
src/rules/      constraint objects, one per H-ID, each independently testable
src/solver/     assign.mjs (Stage A), place.mjs (Stage B), cost.mjs, reporting.mjs
src/validate/   adapter to conflict tools/, the independent H1–H20 checker
src/export/     CSV / XLSX / PDF writers; per teacher, room, group, subject views
src/api/        Express routes + BeggarDev mock: timetable CRUD + solve job + status
src/App.jsx     React/Vite views; the review page displays rubric scores, never recomputes them
```

- **Local:** CLI — `solve --data data.xlsx --out out/` with a human-readable report.
- **Mock:** fixture JSON backend exposing the same endpoints the website will call, so
  the UI can be built and demoed without a production server (BeggarDev's scope).
- **Website:** read-only student timetable lookup plus an admin view of teacher/room
  timetables.

Validator is deliberately **separate from the solver** — the solver must never be the
only thing that says the schedule is valid.

## Operation

The system operation is divided into three stages: **Input → Process → Output**. This section defines the work path for the larger implementation agent. Begin with Input; Process follows after its data contract is agreed, and Output follows the process design.

Cross-stage build order with per-step gates: [`development-journey.md`](development-journey.md).

### A. Input — source data to validated timetable data

**Goal:** Take the supplied PDF guideline(s) and XLSX workbook, extract and normalize their contents, then produce validated JSON for the Process stage. Keep the original values traceable; never silently invent missing assignments or resolve disputed source data.

**Work path for the implementation agent:**

1. **Inventory the source files.** Identify the XLSX sheets/columns and the PDF guideline documents to ingest. Record file names and source locations (sheet and row for workbook records; page and extracted text reference for PDF rules) so every normalized value can be traced back.
2. **Extract PDF text with `pdftotext`.** Use `pdftotext -layout` to create a plain-text intermediate for each source PDF. Keep the extracted text as a reproducible intermediate, capture command failures, and report when text is missing or garbled (for example, a scanned PDF that needs OCR). Do not silently skip a PDF or treat failed extraction as an empty rule set.
3. **Read the XLSX workbook in plain JavaScript.** Import each relevant sheet into raw records. Keep XLSX reading and schema mapping separate so future website/API input can reuse validation without depending on the workbook parser.
4. **Agree and document the canonical JSON schema.** Use the entities in §3 (`Level`, `Group`, `Subject`, `Teacher`, `Room`, `Timeslot`) as the starting point. Include structured scheduling rules extracted from PDF text, with rule ID/text and PDF page provenance. Define stable IDs, required/optional fields, accepted enum values, group participation, fixed pins, and source references. Keep the schema in one versioned JavaScript module and document its JSON shape.
5. **Map PDF and workbook data into JSON.** Combine workbook records and explicitly structured PDF rules into a versioned input JSON package for the Process stage. Keep source-reading, extraction, and schema-mapping code separate. Preserve original values and provenance in the JSON; store the extracted PDF text separately as an audit artifact rather than forcing unparsed text into solver fields.
6. **Normalize only unambiguous formatting.** Trim surrounding whitespace, standardize subject-code spacing, and map known weekday spellings (such as `พฤหัส` and `พฤหัสบดี`) to one canonical day. Preserve the original text alongside normalized values or in source metadata. Do not auto-correct conflicting room labels or infer teacher-to-subject assignments.
7. **Validate at the input boundary.** Check required fields and types, valid period counts and day/period ranges, supported subject/room categories, duplicate IDs/codes, references to known levels/groups/rooms, and malformed pins or availability windows. Check that extracted rule IDs and required PDF rules are represented. Separate blocking errors from warnings so source conflicts remain visible without corrupting otherwise usable records.
8. **Report known source questions.** Explicitly flag the printed-vs-row-sum hour discrepancies, missing teacher↔subject assignments, missing room metadata, unspecified clock times, and the general-room label swaps listed in §2.1. Do not treat the existing assumptions in this draft as confirmed department decisions.
9. **Write deterministic outputs.** Produce the canonical JSON package and a human-readable import report containing source files, counts, provenance, normalization actions, errors, and warnings. Re-importing unchanged sources should produce the same canonical JSON and report ordering.
10. **Demonstrate the input contract.** Provide a documented local Node.js command that accepts the PDF and XLSX paths, runs `pdftotext` for PDFs, imports the workbook, validates the combined JSON, and reports actionable failures. The command must work without the website or remote API.

**Input-stage deliverables:** versioned schema documentation/module, PDF text extraction step, XLSX reader, PDF/workbook-to-canonical-JSON importer, boundary validation, representative JSON package, retained PDF text intermediates, import report, and a short usage note. Record unresolved source questions for BeggarDev/the project owner instead of choosing values silently.

**Input-stage acceptance:** all workbook rows and required PDF rules are accounted for; canonical JSON preserves source references; failed PDF extraction is reported; invalid data is rejected with actionable field/source locations; known ambiguities appear as warnings or unresolved questions; and a second import of unchanged inputs yields identical JSON.
